11 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: [1] Large-scale crustal deformation in the Levant is mainly related to the DST and the CFS.
12 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: The former is an active left lateral transform, bounding the Arabian plate and the Sinai
13 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: sub-plate, and the latter branches out of the former and separates the Sinai sub-plate into two
14 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: tectonic domains. In this study we obtain the velocities of 33 permanent GPS stations
15 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: and 145 survey stations that were surveyed in three campaigns between 1996 and 2008.
16 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: We use a simple 1-D elastic dislocation model to infer the slip rate and locking depth along
17 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: various segments of the DST. We infer a 3.1–4.5 mm/yr slip rate and a 7.8–16.5 km locking
18 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: depth along the DST north of the CFS, and a slip rate of 4.6–5.9 mm/yr and locking
19 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: depth of 11.8–24 km along the Jericho Valley, south of the CFS. Further south, along the
20 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Arava Valley we obtain a slip rate of 4.7–5.4 mm/yr and a locking depth of 12.1–23 km. We
21 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: identify an oblique motion along the Carmel Fault with 0.7 mm/yr left-lateral and
22 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 0.6 mm/yr extension rates, resulting in N-S extension across the Carmel Fault. This result,
23 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: together with the decrease in DST slip velocity from the Jericho Valley to the segment north
24 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: of the CFS, confirms previous suggestions, according to which part of the slip between
25 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Arabia and Sinai is being transferred from the DST to the CFS.
