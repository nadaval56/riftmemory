233 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Finding the Distance to the Epicenter
234 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
235 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                                            | Use the time difference between the arrival of the P and S waves to estimate the distance
236 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                                              from the earthquake to the station. (From Bolt, 1978.)
237 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
238 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Measure the distance between the first P wave and the first S wave. In this case,
239 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               the first P and S waves are 24 seconds apart.
240 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
241 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Find the point for 24 seconds on the left side of the chart of simplified S and P
242 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               travel time curves and mark that point. According to the chart, this earthquake's
243 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               epicenter was 215 kilometers away.
244 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
245 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Measure the amplitude of the strongest wave. The amplitude is the height (on paper) of the strongest wave. On this seismogram, the amplitude
246 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               is 23 millimeters. Find 23 millimeters on the right side of the chart and mark that
247 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               point.
248 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
249 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Place a ruler (or straight edge) on the chart between the points you marked for the
250 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               distance to the epicenter and the amplitude. The point where your ruler crosses the
251 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               middle line on the chart marks the magnitude (strength) of the earthquake. This earthquake had a magnitude of 5.0.

253 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Finding the Epicenter
254 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
255 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: You have just figured out how far your seismograph is from the epicenter and how strong
256 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                            the earthquake was, but you still don't know exactly where the earthquake occurred.
257 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                            This is where the compass, the map, and the other seismograph records come in.
258 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
259 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                                            | The point where the three circles intersect is the epicenter of the earthquake. This
260 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                                              technique is called "trilateration." (Image from IRIS.)
261 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
262 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Check the scale on your map. It should look something like a piece of a ruler. All
263 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               maps are different. On your map, one centimeter could be equal to 100 kilometers or
264 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               something like that.
265 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
266 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Figure out how long the distance to the epicenter (in centimeters) is on your map.
267 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               For example, say your map has a scale where one centimeter is equal to 100 kilometers.
268 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               If the epicenter of the earthquake is 215 kilometers away, that equals 2.15 centimeters
269 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               on the map.
270 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
271 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Using your compass, draw a circle with a radius equal to the number you came up with
272 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               in Step #2 (the radius is the distance from the center of a circle to its edge). The center of the circle
273 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               will be the location of your seismograph. The epicenter of the earthquake is somewhere
274 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               on the edge of that circle.
275 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: 
276 [עמ׳ PDF 1]: Do the same thing for the distance to the epicenter that the other seismograms recorded
277 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               (with the location of those seismographs at the center of their circles). All of the
278 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               circles should overlap. The point where all of the circles overlap is the approximate
279 [עמ׳ PDF 1]:                               epicenter of the earthquake.
